# Campus Water Operations & Sustainable Management Knowledge Base (Local KB)

## Overnight Anomalies & Flapper Leaks
During overnight hours (typically 00:00 to 05:00), campus buildings experience minimum legitimate occupancy. Sustained elevated consumption during these hours is frequently caused by malfunctioning toilet flappers, continuously running urinals, stuck solenoid valves, or auto-flush sensor loops. Maintenance teams should isolate washroom riser branches, perform dye-tablet tests in cisterns, and inspect fixture flush valves.

## Sudden Water Consumption Spikes
Abrupt surges in hourly telemetry can indicate scheduled maintenance flushing, fire suppression testing, cooling tower blowdown cycles, laboratory equipment washouts, or catastrophic fixture failures. Always cross-check facility management work logs and event schedules before initiating emergency pipe excavation. Verify meter pulse counters to eliminate electrical transmission noise.

## Gradual Consumption Drift & Pipe Weeping
A progressive upward trend in hourly consumption over 12 to 48 hours often signals underground distribution weeping, pinhole pipe corrosion, degrading pressure-relief valves, or unmonitored HVAC chilled-water loop expansion. Facilities teams should conduct acoustic leak correlation, check pressure gauge drops across isolation zones, and schedule ultrasonic flow verification.

## Storage Tanks & Float Valve Overflows
Rooftop and underground intermediate storage tanks are equipped with mechanical float valves and overflow warning pipes. A submerged or misaligned float arm causes continuous overflow directly to stormwater drains without triggering local user complaints. Inspect storage tank overflow lines, check ultrasonic level sensors, and service mechanical float assemblies.

## Automated Irrigation & Landscape Management
Landscape irrigation systems represent major seasonal water draws. Anomalies often result from stuck irrigation control valves, broken sprinkler heads, rain-sensor bypasses, or outdated seasonal timer programs. Irrigation audits should verify that watering occurs strictly during early morning hours (04:00 - 06:00), check soil moisture sensors, and verify zone solenoid shutoffs.

## HVAC & Cooling Tower Makeup Water
Central cooling towers lose water through evaporation, drift, and blowdown. Excessive makeup water usage indicates excessive blowdown rates, bio-fouling causing scaling and heat transfer loss, or stuck makeup float valves. Regularly analyze water conductivity, monitor cycle of concentration (CoC), and verify automated blowdown controllers.

## Meter Verification & Sensor Calibration
Telemetry meters (electromagnetic or ultrasonic flow meters) can drift over time due to mineral accumulation, low battery voltage in pulse loggers, or pipeline air pockets. Before undertaking invasive plumbing repairs, perform secondary strap-on ultrasonic verification, inspect signal cabling, and verify meter calibration records.

## Decision Support & Human Verification Protocol
AquaGuard AI alerts are operational decision-support indicators derived from statistical and machine learning evaluation of aggregate consumption telemetry. Anomaly detection does NOT constitute physical confirmation of a pipe rupture or leak. Human facility staff must physically inspect the target site, cross-reference work logs, and confirm physical symptoms prior to taking invasive corrective actions. Autonomous water shutoff is strictly prohibited.

## Sustainable Campus Water Management (SDG 6)
Aligning campus infrastructure with UN Sustainable Development Goal 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) involves continuous water auditing, sub-metering high-draw facilities (laboratories, kitchens, athletic centers), installing low-flow aerators, harvesting rainwater for landscape irrigation, and fostering student water conservation awareness.
